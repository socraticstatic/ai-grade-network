NaaS · Who it serves

Nine roles.
One table, nine shapes.

NaaS puts a customer's whole network on one table: the four layers of the stack, from the access circuit to the AI endpoint, and what they do with each. Every role that touches it lives somewhere on that table. The footprint beside each persona is that somewhere.

What we're building

Day zero, then day one.

NaaS carries a customer from the first connection through every day after it. The storefront's rail follows the customer: which one they see is decided by what is already on AT&T, not by the page they are standing on.

Day zero

How and where do I connect?

A line with an end: get connected.

  • DiscoverSee the estate as it is: clouds, sites, business units, and the sources that find them.
  • ConnectThe best way onto AT&T for each site and region, compared, then ordered.

Day one

A loop: keep it right.

  • ObserveTraffic, the health of every path, capacity, what changed, and the findings, money first.
  • GovernWhere policy is enforced, and where it is only written.
  • CostWhat to change first, and where every dollar goes.

The personas

Who uses NaaS, and what they do with it.

A discovered set, each cited to its research. Six roles run the network today. Two arrived with the token layer and the egress bill. The ninth, the platform engineer consuming the network as an API, comes straight from where TM Forum and GSMA CAMARA say NaaS is going: into the deploy pipeline.

45 jobs: 18 on day zero, 27 on day one. 41 are built into NaaS today, each named by the screen it happens on; 4 are still to build.
